Skin Profile upgrade for AI suggestions

Today's Skin Profile captures 12 basic fields. That isn't enough for safe, personalised AI suggestions. This proposal adds clinical depth like Fitzpatrick phototype, sun behaviour, structured reactions, and active tolerance. It also adds an optional Medical & Safety section for pregnancy, conditions, medications, and recent procedures. Sensitive data is encrypted at rest. Nothing in the form ever feels mandatory.

3 · Medical & Safety consent dialog

Plain language. Equal-weight buttons.

When you tap "Add Medical & Safety", this dialog opens. The content is layered: a plain summary first, a "How is this protected?" panel below, and a link to the full Privacy Notice. Both buttons are the same size. There is no Accept trap.

Before you share medical context

This is sensitive data. Read what we'll ask, why, and how it's protected before you decide.

What we'll ask

  • Skin conditions like rosacea or eczema. Different routines work better.
  • Medications affecting skin. Some interact with skincare.
  • Recent procedures like peels or laser. Your skin barrier may need gentler care.

Design principles applied

Why every screen looks the way it does

  • Two-track setup. Track 1 (Essential, 5 steps, about 2 min) gets you into the app fast. Sensitive sections live after onboarding as opt-in cards.
  • Equal-weight buttons everywhere. "Not now" and "Maybe later" match the primary button in shape, size, and font weight. Only the fill differs. There is no Accept trap.
  • Layered consent. Plain language first, protection details on demand, full legal copy via link. You choose your depth.
  • "Prefer not to say" everywhere. Per question, not just per enum. Even when "None" exists.
  • "Why we ask" on every health question. One tap reveals the specific reason.
  • Encryption is shown. A small violet 🔒 badge sits on every consent-gated card and section, so you see how it's protected before you tap.
  • Honest framing. Optional sections describe what they add if filled. They never blame you for skipping.
  • Consent Center. Withdraw as easily as you grant (GDPR Art. 7(3)). See what's stored (Art. 15). Download or delete everything (Art. 17, 20).
What's deliberately not in these mockups. No completion-streak badges on health sections (coercive). No "85% of users complete this" social proof on sensitive sections (also coercive). No red dots demanding attention. No urgency timers. Sensitive data collection should feel invited, not chased.
